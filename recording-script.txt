FORGE recording script, read naturally, at your normal pace, in a quiet room.
Takes about 10 minutes to read aloud. The goal is variety: questions, statements,
numbers, names, short and long sentences. Don't perform,  just talk like yourself.

(https://cloud.google.com/text-to-speech#put-text-to-speech-into-action)

(English United States)

(Read aloud in a fluid, conversational tone.)

--- set 1: warm-up, short sentences ---
The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.
Pack my box with five dozen liquor jugs.
How vexingly quick daft zebras jump.
Bright vixens jump; dozy fowl quack.
Two driven jocks help fax my big quiz.
The five boxing wizards jump quickly.
Jackdaws love my big sphinx of quartz.
Weave a circle round him thrice.
A very bad quack might jinx zippy fowls.

--- set 2: questions ---
What time is it, and where did I leave my keys?
Can you hear me clearly from over there?
Why does the wind sound different at night?
How many miles have we walked today?
Would you like coffee, or would you prefer tea?
Is this microphone actually turned on?
Do you remember what we talked about yesterday?
Where should we go for dinner tonight?
Have you ever seen the ocean in winter?

--- set 3: numbers, names, everyday speech ---
My phone number is five five five, zero one, three seven.
The meeting starts at nine thirty sharp, don't be late.
I bought twelve apples, three loaves of bread, and a gallon of milk.
Sarah, Mike, and Jonathan are coming over on Friday.
The address is 742 Evergreen Terrace, apartment 5B.
It costs forty-nine dollars and ninety-nine cents.
We left at seven fifteen and arrived just before noon.
Call me back when you get this message, please.

--- set 4: longer, flowing sentences ---
There's something deeply satisfying about building a thing with your own hands and knowing that every part of it answers to you and no one else.
The rain started just after midnight, tapping softly against the window while the house settled into its familiar nighttime creaks and sighs.
I spent the whole afternoon in the workshop, losing track of time the way you do when the work is interesting enough to swallow the hours whole.
She told me once that the secret to a good story is knowing which details to leave out, and I've been turning that over in my head ever since.
The old truck coughed, sputtered, and finally roared to life, and we cheered like we'd just won something far more important than a working engine.

--- set 5: emotion and emphasis ---
I can't believe it actually worked!
No. Absolutely not. Not this time.
Listen,  this part is important, so pay attention.
Wow, that sounds incredible. Say it again.
I'm telling you, it was the best day of the entire year.
Stop. Wait. Did you hear that?
Yes! Finally! That's exactly what I wanted.
Hmm, I'm not so sure about that idea.

--- set 6: cool-down, conversational ---
So anyway, that's the whole story, more or less.
Let me know what you think when you get a chance.
I'll be around all afternoon if you want to talk it through.
Thanks for listening,  I know that was a lot.
Alright, I'm going to grab some water and stretch my legs.
We'll pick this up again tomorrow, same time.
Take care of yourself, and don't work too late.
Goodnight, and sleep well.
